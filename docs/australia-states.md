# Australia — states

Only once you know it is Australia.

## Signs

| See this | State |
|---|---|
| **Red** "No Stopping" sign | **NSW** — all other states use white |
| White "No Stopping" / "No Parking" **with text** | **ACT** |
| **White** direction sign | **TAS** |
| **Fluorescent** border on a road sign | **QLD**, almost exclusively |
| **Flat** signpost — the post, not the sign | **SA or WA**, usually **yellow** in WA |
| Yellow **stickers** on the signpost | **NT** |
| Road number starting with **C** | **VIC or TAS** (also SA near the VIC border) |
| 130 km/h speed limit | **NT** |
| **Yellow** licence plate | **NSW** |

**Careful:** SA uses **rounded** posts too. A round post never rules SA out — only a flat one
rules the other states out.

**Mnemonic:** flat post = the two "-A" codes, S**A** and W**A**. Yellow **plate** = east (NSW),
yellow **pole** = west (WA).

## Poles

| See this | State |
|---|---|
| **Stobie pole** — steel rails with a concrete slab between ("sandwich"), trident or rotated-E top | **SA** |
| Rusty metal pole **full of holes** | **NT** |
| **Trident-like** top | **WA or VIC** |
| **Green painted base**, sometimes a white sticker or a red-and-white band | **WA** |
| Three **circular coils** — two at the sides, one upright on top | **VIC** |
| Coils **angled upwards** at the sides | **QLD** |
| Circular **blue sticker** | **QLD** |
| Black markings with diagonal numbers | **QLD** — Gold Coast, Brisbane, Sunshine Coast |
| Thin metal crossbar bent 90° into an **L** | **TAS** — normal Australian crossbars are wood |
| **Olive-green** possum guard | **TAS** — every state has guards, the colour decides |

**Careful — three exceptions worth knowing:**

- The trident top only counts if **all three insulators sit on the crossbar**. Middle insulator on
  the pole itself = different pole, no meta. In WA it often comes with red insulators and a black
  painted crossbar.
- Stobie poles are SA, but a few thousand stand in other states, most notably **TAS**.
- Holey poles are NT, but also turn up in **northern WA** (e.g. Wyndham).
